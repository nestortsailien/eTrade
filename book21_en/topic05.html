🎓 Degrees and Credentials:
What Are They For?

Do you truly need a degree, or do you need the expertise, perspective, and opportunities that education can provide?

📜 A Degree Is a Signal

A degree is not the same as capability, but it is not meaningless paper either. It can signal preparation before others know your work directly.

🧠 Credential vs. Capability

A diploma cannot guarantee that you can solve problems, complete work, or create value. Evidence of learning and actual ability are different assets.

⏳ The Real Cost Is Opportunity Cost

Graduate school costs more than tuition. It also uses years that could have gone into work experience, projects, income, or other forms of learning.

🔬 Three Values to Separate

Professional depth
Broader perspective and research training
The formal credential itself

🚪 Some Fields Require the Door Key

In some professions a formal degree is a threshold. In others, portfolios, practical ability, and demonstrated results matter far more.

💡 Education Is an Investment

The best education investment increases your capability, perspective, and range of options together—not merely the attractiveness of a certificate.

⚖️ Do Not Worship or Dismiss Degrees

“Everyone needs one” and “degrees are useless” are both oversimplifications. The right judgment depends on your goals, industry rules, costs, and alternatives.

🔎 Better Questions

Is a degree a requirement, an advantage, or mostly irrelevant in my target field?
What resources could school give me that work or self-study cannot?
If I do not enroll, how will I build equivalent or stronger evidence?

✨ Takeaway

A degree may open a door, but how far you go depends on what you can do after the door opens.
