🚪 What If You Chose
the Wrong Major or Job?

A poor early choice does not create a lifelong obligation. The useful question is whether this path is still worth continuing from today onward.

🧭 Past Choices Are Information, Not Obligations

An earlier decision can teach you something without permanently defining what you must do next.

💸 The Sunk-Cost Trap

Years of study, work, money, and effort can make leaving feel wasteful. But those costs have already occurred whether you stay or go.

🔮 Judge the Future, Not the Past

The next decision should be based on expected future value rather than a need to justify what you already invested.

🛤️ An Early Choice Is Not a Lifetime Contract

A major, a first job, or even an entire career chapter can be part of exploration rather than a permanent identity.

🧱 Persistence Is Not Automatically Virtuous

Staying can be meaningful accumulation or fear of admitting a mismatch. Leaving can be avoidance or intelligent loss-cutting. Context decides.

👥 Other People’s Expectations

Family, peers, and social norms can matter, but they should not replace your own judgment about fit and future value.

🎒 Carry Skills Forward

Changing direction does not erase what you learned. Skills, judgment, relationships, and experience can often travel into the next path.

🔎 Better Questions

If I were choosing today, would I still enter this path?
Am I staying because the future is worthwhile or because I already invested a lot?
What capabilities from this experience can move with me?
If I leave, what am I moving toward—not only away from?

✨ Takeaway

Life is not a multiple-choice test you can answer only once; what matters is keeping the ability to choose again.
